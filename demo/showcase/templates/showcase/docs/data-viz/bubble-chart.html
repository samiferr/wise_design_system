Use cases

  • A scatter relationship where a third variable also matters - price vs units sold, sized by total revenue.
  • Comparing three numeric dimensions at once without adding a second chart or a color-coded legend for the third value.
  • Highlighting which points in a scatter are most significant by literal visual weight (bigger bubble = bigger value).
  • Not for more than roughly a dozen points before overlapping bubbles become hard to read - consider bucketing or a different chart at that point.

fill_alpha gives every bubble translucency, not just an area fill under a line - bubbles overlap by nature, and opaque ones hide each other entirely.

Size is scaled by area

Chart.js takes a bubble's r as a literal pixel radius - it does not scale it for you the way it does a polar-area slice. bubble_points() does that scaling by hand: radius is proportional to the square root of the value, so doubling the radius (quadrupling the visible area) requires a 4x value, not a 2x one - the honest reading, since the eye judges a bubble's area, not its radius. 

{% filter force_escape %}{% verbatim %}charts.bubble_points([(1, 1, 1), (2, 2, 4)], max_radius=10)
# [{'x': 1, 'y': 1, 'r': 5.0}, {'x': 2, 'y': 2, 'r': 10.0}]
# a 4x value gives a 2x radius, hence 4x the area{% endverbatim %}{% endfilter %}

Three variables is a lot

{% lucide "alert-triangle" size=16 cls="callout-icon" %}
Position is read accurately; area is not. If the third variable is the one that matters, put it on an axis and use a scatter or bar chart instead — reserve bubbles for when size is genuinely secondary context.
