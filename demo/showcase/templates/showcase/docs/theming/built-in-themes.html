What actually changes

The trick that makes dark mode cheap is inverting the neutral ramp: gray-50 becomes the darkest tone and gray-950 the lightest. Every existing text-gray-600 or bg-gray-100 in the component layer and in your own templates then lands the right way up with no markup changes.

The on-colors

A primary button in light mode is white text on dark green. Dark mode lightens the fill for contrast against a near-black page — at which point white text would fail. So components paint their label with --color-on-action rather than a hardcoded text-white, and the theme flips it to a near-black.

Badge 
{% filter force_escape %}{% verbatim %}.btn-primary {
    @apply bg-action-600 border-action-600 hover:bg-action-700;
    color: var(--color-on-action);   /* not text-white */
}{% endverbatim %}{% endfilter %}

Writing your own filled component?
Use var(--color-on-action) for its label. Hardcoding text-white works in light mode and becomes unreadable in dark.

Surfaces

Raised surfaces use --color-panel, not white. Keeping those separate is what lets the surface darken while white stays available as a real white for the few things that need one.
